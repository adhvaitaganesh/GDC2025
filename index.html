DATASET OVERVIEW

Where the responses come from

The analysed corpus consists of English-language response records from the Global Dialogues survey data, including responses translated into English in the source files. Country is taken from respondent metadata. Response records are not unique individuals: the 805 participants contributed multiple answers.

METHODOLOGY

From open-ended responses to exploratory measures

The analysis uses a defined codebook and transparent aggregation rather than assuming that a single score can represent what a country thinks about AI.

01 · PREPARE

Survey responses

Retain Ask Opinion items; pair response text with its question and extract respondent country from metadata.

02 · CLASSIFY

Question-aware NLI

Use a Hugging Face entailment model for seven non-exclusive themes (multi-label threshold 0.70).

03 · SUMMARIZE

Sentiment and themes

Estimate response tone with VADER and calculate theme-mention distributions for each country.

04 · COMPARE

Country-level indices

Compute AIOI, EAI, GECI and DSS and examine exploratory correlations with contextual indicators.

What the four indices measure

AIOI (−100 to +100): positive-response share minus negative-response share; EAI (0–1): economic threat mentions divided by economic opportunity plus threat mentions; GECI (0–1): governance and ethics mentions as a share of all theme mentions; DSS (0–1): normalized Shannon entropy of the seven-theme distribution. Despite its legacy name, DSS measures thematic diversity, not reasoning sophistication.

Interpretation limits

The coded themes are model outputs, not human-validated labels. Multiple themes may be assigned to one response, so theme shares describe mentions, not mutually exclusive respondent views. Country-level indicators summarize this set of participants and are not nationally representative. Correlations are based on just 9–10 country observations.

INTERPRETATION

What the current analysis supports

The most informative result is not confirmation of a single theory: it is a more qualified account of which expectations find support in this small cross-country comparison.

Development and governance

Governance-and-ethics salience does not increase consistently with development. For HDI versus GECI, Pearson r = −0.249. This does not support a simple hierarchy of concerns.

Technological readiness and diversity

Government AI readiness rank shows only a small association with DSS (Pearson r = +0.206). The exploratory index does not measure the quality or sophistication of reasoning.

Economic anxiety and civic freedom

EAI and Freedom in the World are positively associated (r = +0.866, unadjusted p = 0.0012). This relationship remains below the 0.05 false-discovery threshold after correction for all 40 tests (q ≈ 0.047). It is observational and does not imply causation.

Research status: The Atlas organizes qualitative discourse using a question-aware NLI pipeline and a deductive codebook. The analysis does not establish nationally representative attitudes, validated psychometric scales, or causal effects of institutional conditions on AI views. The findings should motivate targeted validation and more representative comparative research.
